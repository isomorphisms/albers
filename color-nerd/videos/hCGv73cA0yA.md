## Physical setup

Donahue uses a transparent iridescent plastic sheet and changes both illumination color and geometry. The apparent contradiction is the point: the sheet can transmit enough light to see through, cast a colored shadow, and become strongly reflective or apparently opaque at another angle.

## Claim ledger

1. He begins under red illumination to make the angular behavior easy to notice.
2. The sheet's apparent transparency or opacity changes with orientation because the optical interference condition changes with angle.
3. He switches to white illumination so the rejected and transmitted wavelength regions can be seen as colors rather than merely as more or less red light.
4. At one geometry the reflected light is cyan-like, which implies that long-wavelength/red contribution is being suppressed in the complementary path.
5. The transmitted/shadow color records a different part of the wavelength-selection story than the specular reflection seen from the sheet itself.
6. Changing angle shifts the interference condition, so a different wavelength band is preferentially rejected; he uses a green-versus-magenta example to show the complementary relation.
7. The lesson is that “what color is this material?” is incomplete for an interference material. The answer depends on illumination spectrum, incidence angle, viewing angle, reflection, and transmission.

## Important distinctions

- **Pigment absorption** and **thin-film interference** are different physical mechanisms even when both can yield complementary-looking transmitted/reflected colors.
- Reflection color and transmission color need not be identical descriptions of the same spectral selection.
- Iridescent appearance is relational to geometry, not a single stored RGB triplet.

## Technical qualification

The short is a qualitative demonstration. A quantitative model would require film thickness/refractive indices, phase differences, polarization where relevant, and spectral/angular response. The note preserves the physical point without pretending the short supplies that full model.

## Albers relevance

This is a strong warning against reducing all color samples to flat swatches. Albers mostly controls ordinary surface-color relations, but any broader computational “color object” should record whether appearance is invariant or view/illumination dependent. Otherwise an iridescent material would be falsely treated as one point in a color space.
