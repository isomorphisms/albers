# How Are Lightness and Brightness Different?

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/5qbtjJe1V2o
- Video ID: `5qbtjJe1V2o`
- Date: 2023-04-03
- Creator: Peter T. Donahue / Color Nerd
- Status: **full-note**

## Claim ledger

Donahue uses a painted cube rather than beginning from terminology. A colored patch is shown while the apparent illumination on its face changes. The patch itself has not changed, but its appearance does because the visual system interprets it relative to the illumination implied by the surrounding cube.

He then alters the context so the same kind of patch, sitting on what appears to be a shadowed face, looks implausibly intense — almost as though it emits light. The demonstration separates two appearance judgments that are often collapsed in ordinary speech.

His teaching distinction is:

- **lightness** concerns how reflective/light a surface appears relative to its inferred illumination;
- **brightness** concerns how intense or luminous the visual stimulus appears.

The important content is the contextual demonstration, not treating a few seconds of spoken definition as a complete CIE glossary.

## Albers connection

This is almost exactly the kind of separation the Albers experiments need: fixed physical/display values do not determine perceived surface appearance by themselves. Illumination cues and surrounding structure are part of the state presented to the observer.

A useful future fixture is therefore: hold a patch value fixed, alter only the surrounding illumination cues, and record when the patch crosses from “light surface” toward “self-luminous/glowing” appearance.
