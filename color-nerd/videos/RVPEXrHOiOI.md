## Thought experiment

Donahue asks what happens to the idea of complementary colors if the observer has four receptor classes rather than the simplified three-channel human model.

## Claim ledger

1. Human complementary pairs are introduced operationally: two stimuli are complementary when their combined receptor stimulation can produce an achromatic/white response under the model.
2. He uses red versus cyan as the familiar three-channel example: one side is treated as one receptor contribution and the other as the remaining two.
3. He then adds a fourth UV-sensitive receptor channel for a hypothetical bird observer.
4. Once there are four channels, ordinary human complement pairs no longer exhaust the possible partitions of the receptor set.
5. Complements can now be organized as **one channel versus three** or **two channels versus two**.
6. To make the inaccessible UV-involving combinations discussable, he invents labels such as “ultra cyan,” “ultra blue,” and “gruv.” These are mnemonic placeholders, not claims that birds use those categories.
7. Human-visible white (three visible-channel contributions) becomes, in the toy construction, the complement of the UV-only component rather than the bird observer's complete white.
8. The broader point is that complementarity is observer-dependent. It is not a relation existing only among wavelength labels independently of the visual system.

## Teaching-model caveat

This is deliberately simplified. Real avian color vision varies by species and includes receptor sensitivities, cone oil droplets, retinal organization, neural opponent channels, adaptation, and ecological specialization. The video's invented names and simple four-channel arithmetic are conceptual scaffolding, not a zoological color standard.

## Mathematical residue

The useful abstraction is not the invented color names but the change in dimensional/combinatorial structure when another independent receptor channel is added. Relations that look exhaustive in a trichromatic response space cease to be exhaustive in a tetrachromatic one.

## Albers relevance

Albers demonstrates that appearance depends on context even for one observer. This video adds a second dependency: the observer itself. Any claim of “same,” “opposite,” or “complementary” should therefore state the observer/model under which the relation is being asserted.
