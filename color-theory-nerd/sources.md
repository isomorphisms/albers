- *Everything TikTok Taught Me About Color Theory*;

- chroma vs saturation;
- lightness vs brightness;

- iridescence;

- RYB vs CMY;
- Ostwald;
- Moses Harris;
- Amy Sawyer;
- Elizabeth Lewis;

It was used as a discovery/checking aid. The files in this directory are **new paraphrases and syntheses**, not copied transcripts. When an exact scientific or historical point matters, follow the video and then its cited primary sources.

## Josef Albers sources

### Josef and Anni Albers Foundation — Interaction of Color

https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching/interaction-of-color

Useful for Albers's perception-first method, his skepticism about beginning with color systems, and the structure of his experimental exercises.

### Foundation — Teaching

https://www.albersfoundation.org/alberses/teaching

Useful for classroom history and surviving student work. The page documents Yale studies made with Color-aid paper, including transparency studies and the "make one color look like two" exercise.

### Foundation — workshops

https://www.albersfoundation.org/learning/workshops

Current hands-on adaptations of exercises from *Interaction of Color*.

### Yale University Press — *Interacting with Color*, Fritz Horstman

https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300272437/interacting-with-color/

A modern practical guide from the Albers Foundation's education director. Particularly useful because it stresses that *Interaction of Color* is better understood as a method of learning to see than as a closed overarching theory.

## Reliability conventions used in this directory

### `verified`

The title/URL and substantial content were recoverable from the original page, an institutional source, or transcript-derived notes.

### `catalog note`

The title/URL were recoverable, but a dependable full transcript was not. Summary is deliberately limited to the documented topic and should be expanded later.

### `teaching model`

Donahue is intentionally simplifying a scientific situation for a short visual explanation. The note preserves the insight but should not be cited as a complete technical model.

## Things deliberately not asserted

- That Donahue is a vision scientist or color scientist by formal academic credential. The sources establish him as an artist/educator/teacher/lecturer and active color-literacy contributor; that is enough.
- That every numerical view count in an older index is current. View counts change and are not important to this research.
- That arbitrary RGB samples from a scan or cheap printing of *Interaction of Color* reproduce the original physical samples accurately.
- That any one wheel — Donahue's included — is the unique scientifically correct representation for every color task.
