# Color Nerd — long-form videos, lectures, and substantial demonstrations

This page favors longer pieces that expose Peter Donahue's reasoning rather than only a single visual fact. Where a full transcript-derived note was available, the summary is correspondingly detailed. Other entries are concise catalog notes and should be expanded when revisited.

## Core lectures

### Everything TikTok Taught Me About Color Theory

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7dXXlyi__tA
- Date: 2022-08-31 upload; webinar hosted by the Colour Society of Australia in July 2022
- Length: about 78 minutes

Donahue looks back on hundreds of short color-theory videos and the misconceptions revealed by audience responses. The important section is his treatment of pigment mixing. He argues that schoolbook "subtractive mixing" is too simple to predict real paint mixtures: paths through color space curve, different starting hues behave differently, and artist complements do not automatically mix to neutral.

He develops an artist-facing model using CIECAM02 hue organization, diagrams of inward- and outward-curving mixture paths, David Briggs's work, Harold Küppers's term "integrated mixing," and Kubelka-Munk as the more rigorous theory sitting behind the pedagogical simplification. He also discusses why people call brown neutral, why inherited complement pairs often fail, and how he iterated his Color Disc in response.

This may be the best single introduction to his overall project: **replace brittle rules with a visual model that is still simple enough to teach.**

### Things to Know about Mixing Paint

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jLSCbbID1ck
- Date: 2022-11-22
- Length: about 52 minutes
- Context: lecture/demonstration for first-year painting students at the University of the Fraser Valley

A particularly useful video for this repository because Donahue is physically mixing acrylic paint rather than discussing color only on a screen.

Topics include:

- why a printed twelve-hue wheel badly distorts perceptual distances;
- actual paint positions in CIE-derived color space;
- nonlinear paint ratios;
- tinting strength;
- absolute versus relative chroma;
- why cadmium red plus cobalt blue makes a dull purple;
- why quinacridone magenta is better for vivid purples;
- how white changes hue and chroma as well as lightness;
- how black plus yellow can move toward olive/green;
- why paint names are not enough to predict mixtures.

The central practical maxim is that **paints are physical substances with quirks; the abstract color name is not the pigment.**

### You're Wrong about Color Harmony

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXDCQjeM8Hk
- Published: 2025-01-21
- Context: edited version of a lecture given to a second-year graphic-design class at Chapman University

Donahue attacks the standard recipe of choosing complementary pairs, triads, and tetrads by drawing geometric intervals on an RYB wheel. His complaint is not that hue relations are irrelevant; it is that hue geometry alone does not do the jobs a design palette actually has to do.

He asks instead whether a combination communicates, carries a mood or character, remains legible, and works for people with different forms of vision. The lecture ends by advocating a **character-first rather than hue-first** approach to harmony, pointing toward Ellen Divers and Shigenobu Kobayashi.

This is especially relevant to Albers because it shifts attention from nominal relationships between isolated swatches to what the colors do together.

### HOW TO USE MY SCIENCE-BASED COLOR WHEEL

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=alrdClpGHvY
- Published: 2022-11-23

An explicit statement of why Donahue built his Color Disc. The familiar wheel, he argues, is based on inherited ideas that neither predict pigment mixtures well nor represent harmony reliably. His research-based disk is an attempt to give artists a more honest map without requiring them to learn a professional color-science workflow.

Read together with *Everything TikTok Taught Me About Color Theory* and *Things to Know about Mixing Paint*: those longer talks explain the mixture-path and perceptual-space reasoning behind the tool.

## Color-wheel and color-system investigations

### The Only Color Wheel I Use

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EACRRXynk1w

A tour of the kind of color wheel Donahue is willing to use after rejecting the ordinary RYB teaching wheel. Useful as an entry point into his criteria for a practical wheel: it should be clear about what it represents, preserve useful perceptual relationships, and not pretend that idealized geometry predicts every pigment mixture.

### Why this Color Wheel has Five Primary Colors

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=06ul1hBUjaE

A discussion of the Munsell system and why a useful color-order system need not begin from the familiar three-primary story. Donahue uses the five principal Munsell hues to pry apart two questions that basic art education often conflates: **how should colors be ordered perceptually?** and **which physical colorants can be used to mix a gamut?**

### The Most Honest Color Wheel I've Seen

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wcsPGiNgJHM

Donahue evaluates a wheel largely by the information it admits rather than by visual neatness. This belongs to the channel's broader argument that a diagram should expose irregularities — gamut limits, unequal intervals, awkward mixture paths — instead of cleaning them away merely to preserve twelve symmetrical wedges.

### This Guy Rebuilt the RGB Color Wheel From Scratch

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mLQeo_sR9Tk

A case study in reconstructing a color wheel from an RGB/perceptual starting point rather than inheriting the RYB template. It is useful for seeing Donahue treat color diagrams as designed models with assumptions, not inevitable pictures of nature.

### Why I love Frans Gerritsen's interactive color wheel

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOs0C-PvRmY

Gerritsen's work is one of Donahue's recurring historical touchstones because it connects subtractive CMY behavior, additive RGB perception, and the physiology of three cone classes. The interactive/overlay character of Gerritsen's diagrams makes the relationships operational rather than merely decorative.

### What Japan Did With Munsell That the West Didn't

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IkMsZ5YFEyo

A historical color-order case study using Japanese adaptations of Munsell. The larger interest is how a supposedly universal color system changes once it enters a different design and educational culture. This is useful beside Donahue's repeated point that color pedagogy has a history rather than a single inevitable lineage.

### I Blame this 1769 Color Wheel for Centuries of Bad Color Theory

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bAIWcrBN6CY

Donahue traces equal 120-degree RYB spacing to Moses Harris's 1769 wheel. His provocative historical thesis is that later teaching inherited the geometry while ignoring the practical evidence in Harris's own work: a vivid red-violet required an additional carmine-lake pigment. In other words, the canonical diagram already contained evidence against treating three RYB pigments as an ideal universal mixing basis.

### Color Wheel? Nah. Color Pentagon!

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJ1xXwr3gtE

Another attack on the assumption that "color wheel" necessarily means a circle divided according to a three-primary story. The pentagonal framing is useful as a reminder that the topology and geometry of a teaching diagram encode a claim about color relationships.

### The Fascinating Color Wheel of Christine Holbrook

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O-n5WeurF3s

One of Donahue's recoveries of a less familiar color theorist/system. It belongs with his work on Amy Sawyer, Elizabeth Lewis, and other figures omitted from the standard Newton–Goethe–Chevreul–Itten storyline.

## Perception and illumination

### Warm Light, Cool Shadow... Or Vice Versa?

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_wRA-XvgFKo

A painter-facing discussion of the familiar warm-light/cool-shadow rule. Donahue's broader temperature work treats warm/cool not as a fixed label attached to a hue but as a systematic spectral and palette shift. The useful question therefore becomes what illumination and surrounding gamut actually do, rather than whether shadows are required to take the opposite temperature adjective.

### The HELMHOLTZ-KOHLRAUSCH EFFECT!

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9UpreD-t6k

Introduces the perceptual effect in which strongly chromatic colors can appear brighter than less chromatic colors of comparable luminance. This is one of the clearest reasons a scalar physical measure of light cannot stand in for all judgments of visual brightness.

## Applied color choice

### Choosing Colors with Confidence

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Flkao2Xa7OI

A practical palette-selection piece. Read it alongside the harmony lecture: Donahue prefers thinking about a palette's perceptual character, contrasts, lightness/chroma structure, and communicative purpose rather than mechanically taking equally spaced points from a wheel.

### The Real Reason Your Palette Has Two of Every Color

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bT7NNuENBOQ

Examines the painter's "split primary" habit — palettes with two reds, two yellows, two blues, often described as warm and cool versions of each. The subject is useful because it exposes the difference between a practical workaround for pigment gamut and a universal theory of six fundamental hues. Donahue's temperature work later gives a more precise vocabulary for what those palette choices actually accomplish.

## Talks outside the ordinary channel format

### Known World Colour 2024 — keynote

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_LxvTxScWWc

A keynote by Donahue on historical/cultural color theory. Catalog descriptions identify the topic as color and cosmic order. It is useful evidence that his work extends beyond short-form debunking into historical lecture material.

### Video on Wojciech Fangor, Color-Aid, and the legacy of Josef Albers

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sb60nHQ--fI

This is the most directly relevant item for the present repository. It connects Fangor and Color-Aid to the pedagogical/artistic legacy of Josef Albers. Keep it paired with [`albers-connection.md`](albers-connection.md); the exact claims should be expanded from a fresh transcript before using it as evidence for Donahue's interpretation of Albers.

## Useful course recordings that are not free YouTube uploads

These are included because they confirm the studio-practice side of his teaching.

### Peter Donahue's Color for Painters

- https://drawingamerica.com/courses/peter-donahues-color-for-painters/
- Six recorded lessons, roughly 1.3–1.7 hours each.
- Uses acrylic-paint exercises and pigment-specific materials to build modern, science-based color concepts for painters.

### Color Temperature for Painters

- https://drawingamerica.com/courses/peter-donahues-color-temperature-for-painters/
- Six recorded lessons.
- Demonstrated in oils; works through spectral bias, warm/cool palette shifts, atmospheric perspective, golden-hour illumination, and multiple light sources.

### Famous Palettes

- https://drawingamerica.com/courses/famous-palettes-with-peter-donahue-caravaggio/
- Four lecture/demo pairs: Caravaggio, Turner, Mary Cassatt, and Munsell.
- Combines historical material, optical/material analysis, and actual painting demonstrations.
