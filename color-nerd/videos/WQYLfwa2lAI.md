# Is Green WARM or COOL?

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/shorts/WQYLfwa2lAI
- Video ID: `WQYLfwa2lAI`
- Date: 2025-06-17
- Creator: Peter T. Donahue / Color Nerd
- Status: **full-note**

## Claim ledger

Donahue explains warm/cool using idealized spectral-reflectance profiles across roughly the visible 400–700 nm range.

He first establishes endpoints: broadly low reflectance appears black; broadly high reflectance appears white. He then sketches a family weighted toward longer wavelengths as the warm side. Moving the profile changes the perceived family through red/orange/yellow and eventually toward neutral extremes. Reversing the spectral bias gives the cool cyan/blue/violet family.

Green does not fit either monotone bias. Its reflectance is concentrated in the middle of the visible range. Shift that concentration one way and the sample becomes bluer; shift it the other way and it becomes yellower. His conclusion is therefore that **green itself is not intrinsically warm or cool**, although particular greens can be shifted warmward or coolward.

He makes the analogous point for purple: because its character involves the two ends rather than one monotone spectral bias, “warm purple” and “cool purple” are shifts from a middle case rather than proof that purple has one fixed temperature.

## Qualification

This is a teaching model for spectral bias, not a complete psychophysical theory of color-temperature judgments.

## Albers connection

Do not attach warm/cool as a fixed categorical tag to an isolated hue. Treat it as a relation produced by a palette, spectrum, illumination, and comparison context.
