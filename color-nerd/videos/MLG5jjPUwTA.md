## Claim ledger

Donahue starts by challenging the headline that researchers simply “discovered a new color.” He uses a Maxwell-triangle-style representation of the relative responses of the three human cone classes and traces how ordinary spectral light moves through the response-ratio space.

The experiment used 488 nm light, but ordinary 488 nm illumination activates more than the M-cone class. The unusual result came from retinally targeted stimulation designed to activate selected M cones while avoiding the L and S cones that normally participate.

To do that, researchers first had to map the cone mosaic in each observer. Cone classes are densely and irregularly interleaved, so the delivery system needs individual retinal coordinates rather than a simple geometric grid.

The resulting experience called **olo** corresponds to a cone-response ratio that ordinary viewing cannot produce. Donahue therefore frames the novelty as access to an otherwise unreachable receptor-response state, not discovery of a new wavelength.

He also stresses a practical limit: targeting is not perfectly isolated. Light can spill onto neighboring L or S cones, so the achieved response does not reach the theoretical extreme of perfectly isolated M-cone stimulation.

## Albers connection

Color coordinates describe an observer response under specified conditions, not an intrinsic one-dimensional property of wavelength. Observer physiology belongs explicitly in any sufficiently ambitious appearance model.
