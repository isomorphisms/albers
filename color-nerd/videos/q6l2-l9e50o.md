## Demonstration and claims

Donahue uses three separate red, green, and blue flashlights. Where all three beams overlap, the illumination appears approximately white: a direct physical demonstration of additive RGB mixing.

He then uses a slit to make the three contributing beams visible separately again. The point is that the apparently white region has not ceased to be composed of the three illumination components.

Next he places an object in the overlapping beams. Because each light arrives from a different direction, the object produces three colored shadows. Each shadow is the region where one illumination component is blocked while the other two remain:

- no red → green + blue → cyan;
- no green → red + blue → magenta;
- no blue → red + green → yellow.

Passing those shadow regions across the slit makes the complementary relation visible: each CMY shadow corresponds to removal of its opposing RGB component.

## Why this matters

RGB and CMY are not arbitrary rival classroom schemes here. The CMY colors arise physically as pairwise remainders of RGB illumination.

## Albers connection

The perceived color of a region depends on the illumination reaching it, not merely on a material label. This is a clean fixture for separating surface color, illumination spectrum, and resulting appearance.
