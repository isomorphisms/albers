## Claim ledger

Donahue explicitly separates color temperature from the familiar perceptual coordinates lightness, chroma, and hue. His complaint is that artists often describe any clockwise/counterclockwise hue change as warming or cooling, which merely renames a hue shift.

His model instead treats a temperature change as a **coordinated transformation of a whole palette**. When a neutral gamut is shifted warmer, different hue regions move differently:

- reds and oranges become somewhat more vivid;
- blues and turquoises lose colorfulness and approach neutral;
- greens move toward yellow while also dulling, so a lime can become olive-like;
- indigos move toward magenta while also losing colorfulness.

Cooling is the corresponding systematic transformation in the other direction. The essential point is that the operation depends on where a color starts; it is not a uniform rotation of a hue wheel.

## Connection to the green video

This supplies the operational version of the claim that green or purple need not be intrinsically warm/cool: temperature describes how an entire gamut is biased, not a permanent adjective assigned to each isolated swatch.

## Albers connection

A temperature experiment should transform a palette coherently and then test appearance, rather than changing one hue coordinate and calling the result “warmer.”
